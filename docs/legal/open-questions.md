| **Q3** | **Pilot artefacts in M0.** The discovery kit collects the pilot's real Excel sheets and notes (`ROADMAP.md §4.1`). Where are they kept, and who can see them? | Keep them on the owner's machine only. Never commit them, paste them into an AI agent, or upload them to a preview. Use anonymized samples for design, and for the M9 import dry-run until the processor agreement is signed | **Now** | D8 in `role-mapping.md`, E015 |
| **Q4** | **Pilot gym's own paperwork.** Does the pilot gym have its own privacy notice for its clients, and an intake form? Should Fivvi provide a short template privacy notice for gyms? | Yes, provide a one-page template in a follow-up PR. Most small gyms don't have one, and they are the controller | **G4** | `privacy-policy.*` §2, terms A7 |
| **Q6** | **Railway DPA execution and health data.** Has Railway signed the submitted DPA, and does its processing schedule cover notes containing health data despite the published Exhibit A listing special-category data as “None”? | Keep the signed copy; obtain written clarification or amended terms before storing health data in notes. Document the US transfer mechanism | **Publish** and **G2** for execution (the privacy policy says we have data protection agreements with every provider, so Railway's DPA must be executed before it is published); **M7** for health notes | `sub-processors.md` §3, ADR-011 |
| **Q8** | **Consent for health notes.** How does the pilot gym get explicit consent (art. 9(2)(a)) before trainers record health information? Option 1: the gym handles it on paper or in its intake. Option 2: add an optional "consent to health data" field and a warning on the note screen (F046–F048) | Option 1 for the pilot; option 2 as a small scope addition to M7 (the owner decides, because it changes feature scope) | **G4** | `dpia-assessment.md` §4, F046–F048 |
| **Q9** | **Minors and vulnerable clients.** Does the pilot gym train clients under 16, or people in rehabilitation, and record health information about them? | If yes, a DPIA becomes effectively required (two WP248 criteria). Ask during the M0 shadow day | **G4** | `dpia-assessment.md` §3.2 |
| **Q12** | **Data protection officer (FG/DPO).** We think neither Fivvi nor the pilot gym needs one: the core activity isn't large-scale processing of special categories. Agree? | Agree; re-check at 2.0 | **Later** | `dpia-assessment.md` §7 |
| **Q16** | **Trainers at the pilot gym.** Are the other trainers employees or freelancers? Do freelancers bring their own clients into the gym's workspace? | If freelancers train their own clients, those clients belong in the freelancer's own workspace (`role-mapping.md` §6.2), not the gym's. Confirm on the shadow day | **G2** | `role-mapping.md` §6 |
| **Q22** | **Who is responsible for a client's own workouts.** A client's own workouts are stored under the studio, but the studio can't see them (owner, 5 and 6 November 2026, [#602](https://github.com/fivvi-app/fivvi/issues/602)). `role-mapping.md` makes the studio the controller of everything stored under it, and names workouts. Is that still right here, or is Fivvi the controller, as for accounts? | Proposal to check: Fivvi is the controller for own workouts, as for accounts, and the documents say so. | **M7** | `role-mapping.md`, `dpia-assessment.md`, `privacy-policy.*`, `retention-policy.md`, #602 |
| **Q23** | **A membership recorded at the desk.** An owner can record a payment outside Fivvi, and the membership starts with no step where the client confirms its terms in Fivvi (owner, 6 November 2026, [#606](https://github.com/fivvi-app/fivvi/issues/606)). Is that enough under Dutch consumer law, also for a bank transfer, where the client may not be at the desk? | Keep it, on two assumptions to check: the agreement is made with the owner directly, and the client gets the terms before agreeing. Seeing them on the agreement afterwards may not be enough. | **G4** | `terms.*`, #606 |
| **Q24** | **Cloudflare Turnstile on the public pages.** It is not in the code yet (E013, #467, M5), so F064 (#642) leaves it off the public sub-processors page and out of the privacy policy, and keeps it as "planned" in `sub-processors.md`. OK, or do you want it announced now? | Leave it off until E013 ships, then add it to the page and the privacy policy in the same PR (steps in `sub-processors.md`, "Planned, not in use yet") | **Later** (E013 ships it, M5) | `sub-processors.md`, `apps/web/site/info/sub-processors.ts`, `privacy-policy.*` §4–§6 |
| **Q25** | **Provider DPAs: Zoho, Sentry, PostHog.** Zoho Mail (our own mailbox, with support, privacy and demo requests) is on the sub-processors page, but its DPA has not been requested or accepted yet (`sub-processors.md` §2). The privacy policy says we have data protection agreements with each provider, and the mailbox gets real demo requests at go-live. Who accepts Zoho's DPA, and when? Also confirm that Sentry's and PostHog's DPAs are accepted (setup #47 is closed, but this was not checked here) | Accept Zoho's DPA through Zoho's DPA form and note the date in `sub-processors.md`; check the Sentry and PostHog acceptances the same way, before the website goes live | **Publish** | `sub-processors.md` §2, `privacy-policy.*` §5–§6 |
| **Q26** | **Nothing cleans up what the privacy policy says is temporary.** No cron task deletes (`apps/cron/src/registry.ts`): (a) sign-in sessions, which keep the raw `ip_address` and `user_agent` (`packages/database/src/schema/auth.ts`, `packages/auth/src/rate-limit.ts`), (b) unused or expired sign-in verification rows, whose identifier holds the email address (a new request for that address replaces the row, `sign-in-plugin.ts`; using the code deletes it), (c) expired rate-limit rows (removed only on a later sign-in or demo request, `queries/rate-limits.ts`). The privacy policy §3.1 and §7 and `retention-policy.md` R2, R10 and R14 now say exactly that, with no time promise. Build the daily clean-up for all three (E014), or stop storing the IP address and user agent, before the policy promises more? | Build one daily E014 task that deletes expired sessions, expired verification rows and expired rate-limit rows, then restore the targets in R2, R10 and R14 and in the policy | **Publish** | `privacy-policy.*` §3.1 and §7, `retention-policy.md` R2, R10, R14, E014 |
| **Q28** | **PostHog's transfer safeguard is not documented.** PostHog EU Cloud stores in Frankfurt, but the DPA and privacy pages we could read name no SCC or Data Privacy Framework certification for access from the US. The privacy policy §6 says "the safeguard in its DPA" for PostHog. Who reads PostHog's signed DPA and names the mechanism, so the policy and `sub-processors.md` §6 can state it? | Download PostHog's DPA from the PostHog app (self-serve signature), read its transfer clause, and name it in the policy and in `sub-processors.md` §6 | **Publish** | `sub-processors.md` §6, `privacy-policy.*` §6 |
